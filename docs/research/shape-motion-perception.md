# Faceless affect: shape, motion and colour at 135 px

Can a creature with **no face** express valence and arousal through silhouette, deformation, motion and colour alone, at ~135×135 px in a 1080p stream?

Markers: **[E]** measured in a primary I read; **[E?]** reported by a secondary that read the primary; **[LORE]** craft practice with no measurement behind it. Unmarked numbers are derived here.

## 1. Shape and affect

**Bar & Neta (2007)**, *Neuropsychologia* 45(10), 2191–2200 — n=16, **85 ms**, 7° visual angle, forced like/dislike **[E]**:

| | sharp | curved | mixed control |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| real objects, % liked | 47.2 ± 3.5 | **62.9 ± 2.6** | 57.4 ± 2.7 |
| novel patterns, % liked | 24.3 ± 4.8 | **34.7 ± 5.6** | — |

`t(15) = −4.82, p < .0005`; no RT difference. Experiment 3 asked about **threat** instead: real objects **42.5% vs 28.0%**, novel patterns 56.8% vs 39.8%, all significant. **The threat half is larger and more robust than the preference half** — which suits us: the goal is "bad state reads as bad", not "viewer likes the buddy".

**Experiment 2 is the key result here.** Split by spatial frequency: low-SF stimuli gave 41.9% vs 58.1%, high-SF gave 53.9% vs 60.8%. **The angularity signal lives in the coarse, blurred image** — precisely the signal that survives downscale, a phone viewport and video compression.

**Speed.** Bar, Neta & Linz (2006), *Emotion* 6(2), n=60 **[E]**: threat rankings at **39 ms** correlate with unlimited-viewing rankings at `r = .774, p < .001`; at 26 ms n.s. Intelligence judgements show no such correlation — the speed advantage is specific to threat. EEG puts triangle-vs-ellipse valence discrimination at **N1, 50–150 ms** (Li et al. 2018, n=20) **[E?]**.

**Aronoff** (1988 *JPSP* 54(4); Aronoff, Woike & Hyman 1992 *JPSP* 62(6), 1050–1066; 2006 *Cross-Cultural Research* 40(1)). Consistent across ceremonial masks, geometric models, ballet and Dutch painting: **acute angles with a downward-pointing vertex read as threat; roundedness reads as warmth** **[E?]**. No primary was obtainable — the direction is well replicated but **no effect size is verifiable**, and the 2006 study's own indexed result is "partially supported".

**Bouba/kiki.** Ćwiek et al. (2022), *Phil Trans R Soc B* 377(1841) — **917 participants, 25 languages** **[E]**: **72% congruent (95% CrI 56–82%)**, and **asymmetric** — *bouba*→round held in **22/25** languages, *kiki*→spiky in only **11/25**. Ramachandran & Hubbard's famous "95%" (2001) has no sample size, no statistics and no methods section; do not cite it. Failures are phonotactic, not perceptual (Hunjara, Syuba); the remote non-literate Himba **do** show it.

**Contested.** The *preference* effect is small and measurement-dependent: 58.3% at 80 ms but **~50% n.s. under unrestricted viewing** (Munar et al. 2015, *PLOS ONE* e0141106); .59/.55/.58 across three cultures (Gómez-Puerto et al. 2018); **reverses on a rating scale** (Maezawa et al. 2020, n=30×4, sharp preferred *d*=0.19). The threat *explanation* has failed direct tests — no peripersonal-distance modulation, curved preferred over straight lines with no angles at all, approach-to-curvature rather than avoidance (Bertamini et al. 2016); **no amygdala increase for angular architecture** (Vartanian et al. 2013, *PNAS* 110(S2)); and with low-level features balanced, **circles are detected faster than downward triangles** (Chuquichambi et al. 2020) — the opposite of Larson, Aronoff & Stearns (2007), whose own downward-V advantage is **[E?]** with no obtainable ms values.

**Takeaway.** Angularity works as a **negative-valence/threat** cue: brief exposure, low spatial frequency, decided under 150 ms, worth ~10 percentage points of judgement. It is not a likeability knob.

## 2. Motion and animacy

**Heider & Simmel (1944)** *Am. J. Psych.* 57(2) **[E]**. The film is **~150 s** (not the 90 s that circulates). N=114: free-description group **33/34** gave an animate reading; "persons" instruction 36/36; reversed film 42/44. A **connected story** came from 19 of 34 free viewers (56%). *Spatial* quantisation barely changes descriptions; *temporal* quantisation drastically reduces animacy. **Motion, not form, does the work.**

**Johansson.** The cited "100–200 ms" is **not** in Johansson (1973), which used **1.0 s** exposure, 10 point lights, a walker subtending **~3°**, and found **five points (hips and legs) sufficient**. The 0.1/0.2 s figures are **Johansson (1976)**, *Psych. Research* 38(4) **[E]**: *all* observers saw a walking person at **0.2 s**, **40% at 0.1 s**. Beintema & Lappe (2002) *PNAS* 99(8): dots at random limb positions reallocated every frame, **four per frame**, still gave 55% recognition — **local image motion is not required; posture sequence suffices.**

**Causal timing.** Michotte's own thresholds are second-hand and a 2025 registered replication did not confirm them. Straube & Chatterjee (2010) *Front. Hum. Neuro.* 4:28, n=16 **[E]**: trials judged **causal** had mean delay **66.9 ms (SD 29.2)**, non-causal **164.7 ms (SD 27.2)**; mean direction change 25.7° for causal. Schlottmann et al. (2013) **[E]**: degrades from ~100 ms, replaced by "two independent movements" by ~200 ms — **but naive observers tolerate a second or more, and practice narrows the window.** One Michotte fact is worth keeping: launching needs the launcher to be the *faster* object (optimum ≈ 3.6:1); if the struck object moves **faster**, the percept flips to *triggering* — its motion reads as **its own**, merely released. **A creature that reacts harder than the event that hit it looks alive; one that reacts proportionally looks shoved.**

**Tremoulet & Feldman (2000)**, *Perception* 29(8), n=24 **[E]**. One object, gravitational frame removed. Entry at 1.71 °/s for **375 ms**, then a **simultaneous** speed and direction change, then **375 ms** more; rated 1–7.

- **Direction change 0/10/20/40/80°** — monotonic, `F(4,92)=9.024, p<.001`.
- **Speed ratio λ = 0.5/1/2/4** — monotonic, `F(3,69)=4.935, p=.004`.
- **Maximum at 80° × λ=4. λ=0.5 (deceleration) rated *below* λ=1 — slowing down reads as dead.**
- **Principal-axis alignment is the largest single effect**, `F(2,46)=124.4`: aligned > dot > misaligned, the misaligned rectangle looking inanimate to most observers **despite a pixel-identical motion path**.

All means fall between **~3.5 and ~4.9 on a 7-point scale** — a reliable ordering, not a switch. Tremoulet & Feldman (2006): a *static* context object raises animacy (`F(4,56)=17.7`) only when the velocity change reads as a **reaction**; a visible paddle that physically explains the change **suppresses** animacy. "Animacy attribution tracks intentionality attribution."

**Contingency is the strongest and cheapest cue.** Johnson, Slaughter & Carey (1998), *Developmental Science* 1(2) **[E]** — contingency = reaction **under 1 second**, on ≥75% of infant behaviours (achieved 90% vs 14% yoked):

| condition | n | gaze-following score |
| --- | ---: | ---: |
| real person | 15 | 1.62 |
| object, +contingent +face | 13 | 1.54 |
| object, **+contingent −face** | 20 | **1.35** (`p<.005`) |
| object, −contingent +face | 13 | 1.18 |
| object, **−contingent −face** | 19 | **−0.46 (n.s.)** |

**Contingency alone matched a face.** The object that moved by itself but did not *react* failed completely; self-generated movement and asymmetric form were "apparently insufficient".

**Jitter is worse than lag.** Haggard, Clark & Kalogeras (2002), *Nature Neuroscience* 5(4) **[E]**: intentional binding shifted the tone **−97 ms** at a fixed 250 ms lag, −35 at 450, −11 at 650 — but only **−32 / +4 / +16 ms when the delay was unpredictable.**

**Chasing psychophysics** — narrow tolerances, measured by performance rather than ratings. Gao, Newman & Scholl (2009), *Cognitive Psychology* 59(2) **[E]**, `F(5,95)=111.54`: **0° deviation from heat-seeking → >85% correct; only 0° and 30° exceed 60%** (chance 50%); ~30 points lost by 60°, then only a third as much to 150°; **120° and 150° are indistinguishable from chance**. Facing direction is worth **~14 points** of escape performance, and only when the shape faces *where it is going*. Gao & Scholl (2011), *JEP:HPP* 37(3) **[E]**: interruptions **shorter than ~100 ms are simply ignored**; **static pauses and jiggling-in-place do not break perceived intent at all — only wandering does**; **60° of deviation impairs detection as much as 180°**.

Gao, McCarthy & Scholl (2010), *Psych. Science* 21(12), Exp 3b bears directly on the existing buddy: a **1.9° white disc with two 0.38° "eyes"** produced the same involuntary avoidance as a sharp-nosed dart (7.97 s vs 9.03 s, `t(6)=5.291, p=.002`, n=7); a sudden-onset flash control produced **none**, so it is not attention capture, and van Buren et al. (2016) **[E?]** show it persists when observers are told to ignore the shapes. **Two dots are a first-class animacy channel — and an attention tax the viewer cannot decline.**

**Ranked:** (1) contingent reaction to an external event; (2) low-deviation goal-directed motion (≤30°); (3) facing direction, including via eyes; (4) simultaneous speed+direction change, peak 80°×4×; (5) principal-axis alignment. **Non-rigid deformation** — Michotte's "caterpillar" displays — is cited in every review but **has never been parametrically studied**. That gap is exactly the channel a squash-and-stretch creature would use.

## 3. Motion parameters → arousal and valence

**Saerbeck & Bartneck (2010)**, *HRI '10*, n=18, 3 (acceleration) × 3 (path curvature) × 2 (embodiment: iCat head vs Roomba floor path) **[E, full data]**:

| DV | acceleration | curvature | embodiment |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Arousal** | `F=114.1, p<.001,` **`ηp²=.870`** | `F=19.5, ηp²=.535` | n.s. |
| **Valence** | **`F=0.755, p=.478` — null** | `F=15.7, ηp²=.481` | n.s. |
| Dominance | `ηp²=.398` | `ηp²=.216` | n.s. |

Stepwise regression: **arousal** on `a², κ², a·κ` → adj. `R²=.471`, `F(3,614)=184.0, p<.001`; **valence** on `a·κ` → adj. `R²=.10`, **`p=0.07`**, which the authors call "not conclusive".

- **Acceleration → arousal is the best-evidenced motion→affect mapping that exists.** Acceleration → valence is a flat null.
- **"Curvature → valence" is not usable**: the means are **non-monotonic** (Roomba: low κ 5.32, **medium κ 4.11**, high κ 4.78 on a 9-point scale).
- **Embodiment had no main effect on anything** — a floor disc and a robot cat head gave identical attributions from identical motion. Direct evidence that a faceless carrier is not handicapped, *for arousal*.

Converging: Roether et al. (2009) *J. Vision* 9(6):15 **[E]** — anger and happiness are both fast and large-amplitude, fear and sadness both slow and small. **Speed and amplitude separate arousal, not valence**, since those four span both valence signs within each kinematic band.

**Valence in motion lives in relative phase.** Pollick, Paterson, Bruderlin & Sanford (2001), *Cognition* 82(2), B51–B61 **[E, abstract; coefficients not obtainable]**: point-light arm movements, ten affects, shown natural **and phase-scrambled**. MDS gave a circumplex (Dim 1 activation, Dim 2 pleasantness); scrambling preserved Dim 1's structure but **not Dim 2's**. Activation is "a formless cue that relates directly to the movement kinematics"; **pleasantness "appears to be carried in the phase relations between the different limb segments."** Amaya, Bruderlin & Calvert (1996) **[E]** reached the same conclusion independently: the **phase shift between joints** is "a distinctive feature of real human motion" and differs between neutral and angry. **This is the valence lever the literature points at, and nobody has tested it on an abstract shape.**

**Bounds.** Minimum-jerk (Flash & Hogan 1985) is the calm floor: `x(t) = A(10τ³ − 15τ⁴ + 6τ⁵)`, peak speed `1.875·A/T`; parameterise agitation as *deviation from* it. Frequency ceiling **~10 Hz** — Amaya et al. found no major spectral components above 10 Hz in neutral or angry wrist motion and low-pass filtering made "no noticeable difference", so **above ~10 Hz reads electronic, not agitated**. Bartram, Ware & Calvert (2003), *IJHCS* 58(5) **[E]** studied 16×16 px moving icons beside a demanding primary task — our exact situation: small periodic motions are **better signals than colour or shape across the entire visual field** and, unlike colour and shape, are **not degraded by eccentricity**; **~1° of visual angle** amplitude is highly detectable and below 1° slows RTs; **1–3 Hz are all effective**; smoothness barely affects detection; and **"motion can contribute strongly to distraction and irritation"**, with *travelling* motions significantly worse than *anchored*. Their recommended default is the **slow anchored linear oscillation**.

**Laban/EMOTE is vocabulary, not evidence.** Morita, Nagai & Moritsu (2013): **"Laban (1980) did not mention any correspondence between the efforts and emotion categories."** Every Effort→emotion table downstream is post-hoc **[LORE]**. Hartmann/Mancini/Pelachaud's six expressivity parameters are rule-authored, not fitted, and **five of the six are monotone in arousal**; only Fluidity has an independent valence claim, with the least evidence.

| Parameter | → Arousal | → Valence | Grade |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Acceleration | **strong +** (ηp²=.870) | **null** (p=.478) | E |
| Speed, peak velocity, jerk | + | none alone | E |
| Spatial amplitude | + | none alone | E? |
| Oscillation frequency 1–3 Hz | + | none | E |
| Smoothness / fluidity | − (smooth = calm) | possibly + | E?/LORE |
| Path curvature | + (ηp²=.535) | **non-monotonic — unusable** | E |
| **Relative phase between parts** | — | **the only supported cue** | E |
| Travelling vs anchored | travelling more salient | — (far more irritating) | E |

## 4. Colour and affect

**Valdez & Mehrabian (1994)**, *JEP:General* 123(4) **[E]**, over Munsell chips:

```
Pleasure  =  .69·Brightness + .22·Saturation
Arousal   = −.31·Brightness + .60·Saturation
Dominance = −.76·Brightness + .32·Saturation
```

**Hue appears in none of them.** Their hue ordering also contradicts folk wisdom: most arousing were green-yellow, blue-green and green; among the *least* arousing was **yellow-red**.

**Wilms & Oberfeld (2018)**, *Psych. Research* 82(5), n=62 **[E, full PDF]** — decisive, because their three hues were **matched in L\* and in luminance Y** (L\*=50 → Y ≈ 40.4 cd/m² for blue, green and red alike). Exactly our constraint.

| | Hue | Saturation | Brightness |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Arousal** | `F=42.8, p<.001,` **`ηp²=.588`** | `ηp²=.693` | `ηp²=.459` |
| **Valence** | `F=3.128,` **`p=.051`**`, ηp²=.094` | `ηp²=.343` (non-mono) | **`ηp²=.491`** |

Valence hue means: green 5.69, blue 5.49, red 5.33 — a **0.36-point spread on a 9-point scale, not significant**. Worse, the saturation × hue interaction (`ηp²=.436`) **reverses the sign**: blue > green > red appears only at high saturation; at low saturation **red rates highest**. Their arousal ratings spanned nearly the IAPS range while "all valence ratings were rather close to the neutral rating category (5)".

| Source | Hue → valence |
| --- | --- |
| Valdez & Mehrabian 1994 | absent from the regression |
| Wilms & Oberfeld 2018 (equiluminant, n=62) | n.s.; sign reverses with saturation |
| Suk & Irtel 2010, *Color Res. Appl.* 35(1) | varies more with tone than hue; chroma `r>.60` |
| Gao et al. 2007, 214 samples, 7 cultures | "influences of hue and cultural background were very limited" |
| Hanada 2018, *Color Res. Appl.* 43(2) | hue circle ≠ circumplex, **hypothesis rejected**; hue dims mediated by *temperature* |
| Jonauskaite & Mohr 2025, 132 studies, 42,266 ppts | **red → high-arousal emotions of *both* valences**; only **light/dark** is a clean valence cue |

**The cross-cultural universals are conceptual, not perceptual.** Jonauskaite et al. (2020), *Psych. Science* 31(10) — **4,598 participants, 30 nations**, pattern similarity `r = .88` — used colour **terms**. The companion study (2021, *PeerJ* 9:e11180) makes the problem explicit: **64 congenitally red-green colour-blind men produced the same associations as 66 controls** (`r=.82` terms, `.80` patches), unmodulated by severity — associations "do not require immediate perceptual colour experiences". **That literature measures a linguistic association; it does not license the inference that a hue on a 135 px blob is read as a feeling mid-fight.**

**Red is not a threat cue.** Gnambs (2020) *Psychon. Bull. Rev.* 27(6), meta-analysis of **67 effect sizes / 38 samples**: `d = −0.06` and `−0.04`; after correcting for publication bias, **"no evidential value"**. Peperkoorn, **Hill**, **Barton** & Pollet (2024), *Sci. Rep.* 14:30822 — the original authors re-analysing **6,589 contests**: **50.5% red wins, not significant**.

**Delivery penalties compound.** Equiluminance **severely impairs motion perception** in random-dot kinematograms (Ramachandran & Gregory 1978, *Nature* 275), so an equiluminant design weakens the *motion* channel that carries arousal. Chromatic acuity is ~2.5× worse than luminance and the chromatic CSF is low-pass, not band-pass (Mullen 1985, *J. Physiol.* 359: ~11–12 c/deg). **4:2:0** (the Twitch/H.264 default) halves chroma per axis — a 135 px widget carries a **~68×68 chroma grid**, and thin coloured edges lose colour identity entirely, surviving only as luminance edges. **CVD** affects ~8% of males of Northern European descent; Dota's audience is overwhelmingly male, so roughly **1 viewer in 12** — red↔green is the worst available pair, and Dota's HUD owns those semantics anyway.

## 5. Small size and low resolution

**Coarse-to-fine.** Schyns & Oliva (1994), *Psych. Science* 5(4) **[E]**: with hybrid images, observers matched the **coarse** component at **30 ms** and the **fine** component at **150 ms**. Hughes, Nozawa & Kitterle (1996) **[E]**: global processing is largely unaffected by conflicting local cues, but **local processing is substantially slowed by conflicting global cues** — a detail that contradicts the silhouette costs you; a silhouette that contradicts a detail costs little.

**Torralba (2009)**, *Visual Neuroscience* 26(1) **[E]**: **32×32 colour → 81.8%** scene categorisation (chance 8.3%), greyscale 73%, 128×128 → 96% (ceiling), 4×4 → chance. Per-image thresholds mode at **11×11 to 22×22 px**; only 3% need more than 90×90. At 32×32 observers named 5 objects at 80% accuracy — but **89% when the scene was correctly identified and only 53% when it was not.** **Our creature is an isolated object on a transparent overlay with no scene context: 53% is the relevant figure.**

**Du & Martinez (2011)**, *J. Vision* 11(13):24 **[E]** — the most actionable number in this document. Expressions from 240×160 down to 15×10 px: happiness and surprise **~95–99%, holding across all resolutions tested**; sadness and anger 63–71% with **no significant loss down to 60×40**; fear and disgust **49–53% even at full resolution** — hard categories, not resolution-limited. Their summary: **"recognition is only impaired in practice when the image resolution goes below 20 × 30 pixels."** Identification needs less still: familiar faces at **7×10 px**, ceiling at **19×27 px** (Yip & Sinha, via Sinha et al. 2006). **This kills one argument outright: "a face is too small at 135 px" is false.** A 135 px body has room for a 40 px eye. The project's finding that a 2–3 px eyebrow reads as noise (issue 04) constrains **sub-features**, not faces.

**Silhouette.** Hayward (1998) *JEP:HPP* 24(2) **[E?]**: stationary-silhouette recognition is roughly comparable to recognition with internal contours, texture and shading. But silhouettes incur **greater viewpoint costs under depth rotation** (Hayward, Tarr & Corderoy 1999 — relevant, ours is 3D geometry), and identifiability ranges **0% to 100% across items** (Wagemans et al. 2008): it is a property of the specific shape, not of silhouettes. The animation "silhouette test" is **[LORE]** — no study shows it predicts small-size legibility.

**Complexity never amortises.** Byrne (1993), *INTERCHI '93*: **simple icons help; complex icons are no better than plain rectangles** **[E?]**. McDougall, de Bruijn & Curry (2000), *JEP:Applied* 6(4): **concreteness effects wash out with experience; complexity effects persist** and stay tied to search efficacy. For something on screen all match, every match: concreteness buys the first minute, complexity charges rent forever.

**Channel bound.** Garner (1974) on separable vs integral dimensions; Healey & Enns (2012) *IEEE TVCG* 18(7) list 16 preattentive cues (including flicker, direction and velocity of motion) at a <200–250 ms criterion, and note that **conjunction targets are generally not detected preattentively**. Hence: each channel is preattentive only **singly**, so any state needing a *conjunction* goes to serial search — for a peripheral overlay that means never read at all. **Two separable channels for two independent dimensions is defensible; three is a stretch; four is unsupported.** (The "3–4 channel limit" often attributed to Ware could not be verified — that literature is about *pairs*.) Extra channels should be **redundant**, correlated with one of the two; Garner predicts *facilitation* for correlated pairs, the only free lunch here. **Motion × colour is near-separable and a good pair; colour × shape is more integral; size_x × size_y — deformation read along two axes as two variables — is explicitly integral.**

**Angular size is the real constraint.** Derived, for 135 px in a 1080p stream:

| Viewing condition | px/deg | creature subtends |
| --- | ---: | ---: |
| 24" 1080p @ 60 cm, fullscreen | ~38 | **~3.6°** |
| same, quarter-size window (~67 px) | ~38 | **~1.8°** |
| phone, ~7 cm wide @ 30 cm | ~144 | **~0.94°** |

At the phone figure the whole creature is under one degree. With Mullen's ~11 c/deg limit that is **at most ~10 chroma cycles across the full width**, so the finest surviving colour feature is **~1/10 of width (~13 px)** — before 4:2:0 halves it again. **Rule: a colour-carried signal must occupy a contiguous region of at least ~1/8 of width (~17 px of 135), never edges, rim lines or coloured detail.** The crisp opaque edges of ADR-0003 are exactly right: luminance-carried, surviving both subsampling and downscale.

## 6. Breathing and idle motion

**Treal et al. (2021)**, *Scientific Reports* 11:12493 **[E, open access]**. Two experiments, n=30 each; idle motion was **motion-captured postural sway including respiration at 50 Hz**, compared against a fully static body.

| | PSE idle | PSE still | JND idle | JND still |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| Exp 1 (pain intensity) | 56.19% | 58.14% (`p=.035`) | **2.78%** | **4.08%** (`p=.033`) |
| Exp 2 (affective response) | 53.9% | 56.9% (`p=.040`) | n.s. | n.s. |

**The JND result is the important one: idle motion *lowered the discrimination threshold* for the character's expression.** It does not only say "alive"; it makes the affective signal easier to read. Effects are modest (~2–3 PSE points) but consistent. A 2026 *Int. J. Social Robotics* study (n=75, crossover) is methodologically ideal — **its control was Perlin-noise movement, not stillness**, so its gains (+10.65 s looking time, +0.69 animacy) would be **breathing-specific rather than motion-generic** — but it is paywalled, so **[E?]**, unverified. Terzioğlu, Mutlu & Şahin (2020) **[E?]**: breathing improved animacy, anthropomorphism, likability and social presence.

**Frequency is physiologically grounded; amplitude is not.** Resting adult respiration 12–20 breaths/min (StatPearls NBK537306; sources disagree, no systematic review found):

| State | breaths/min | **Hz** | period |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| deep rest / sleep | 10–14 | **0.17–0.23** | 4.3–6.0 s |
| resting | 12–20 | **0.20–0.33** | 3.0–5.0 s |
| elevated / stressed | 20–30 | **0.33–0.50** | 2.0–3.0 s |
| agitated | 30–40 | **0.50–0.67** | 1.5–2.0 s |

A clean **~3× span**, grounded rather than arbitrary. Amplitude is weaker ground: quiet-breathing chest-wall excursion is roughly **1–8 mm** against a torso depth of ~200–250 mm, i.e. **~1–4% of body dimension** (bracketed from plethysmography validation ranges, not read directly). **Derived:** 1–4% of 135 px is **1.4–5.4 px** — sub-threshold at 0.94° and destroyed by the encode. **Literal physiological amplitude is too small; exaggerate 2–4×.**

**The cost side.** Bartram: "motion can contribute strongly to distraction and irritation", travelling far worse than anchored. Franconeri & Simons (2003) **[E]**: **translating and looming stimuli capture attention; receding stimuli do not** — growth is expensive, shrinking is cheap. With Gao & Scholl (pauses and jiggling preserve intent, wandering destroys it): **breathing amplitude should fall toward zero at low arousal rather than sit at a constant floor.**

**Loops.** Atxa Landa et al. (2026), *Computer Animation & Virtual Worlds*, **n=123**, thirty 10-second clips **[E]**: participants **could not** distinguish genuine from *acted* idle motion, but **could reliably distinguish hand-made library idles from motion-captured ones** — hand-authored idle reads as authored within ten seconds. **No research exists on how long before a viewer detects an animation loop**; if someone quotes a number, ask for the citation. The usual fixes (randomised phase, Perlin noise, layered desynchronised sinusoids with incommensurate periods) are **[LORE]**, with Egges, Molet & Magnenat-Thalmann (2004) as the one published *method*.

## What this means for a 135 px creature

### Channel budget: two, plus redundancy

At under 1° on a phone, with 4:2:0 chroma and no scene context: **two separable channels carrying the two axes, everything else redundant.**

| Axis | Primary channel | Redundant support |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Arousal** | acceleration / motion magnitude | breathing frequency, deformation amplitude, jerk |
| **Valence** | silhouette angularity (low-SF) | lid/shape geometry, deformation phase coherence |

### Numbers

- **Arousal → acceleration** — the only motion→affect mapping with a large, replicated effect (ηp² = .870). Let speed, amplitude and jerk co-vary.
- **Calm floor = minimum-jerk easing** (`10τ³ − 15τ⁴ + 6τ⁵`); agitation is deviation from it, not a separate animation.
- **Breathing 0.17 Hz (calm) → 0.50 Hz (agitated)**, never above ~0.67 Hz.
- **All oscillation below ~10 Hz** — above that reads electronic, not agitated.
- **Idle amplitude 3–12% of the body (4–16 px)** — physiological 1–4% exaggerated 2–4× to clear the encode — **scaling to zero at minimum arousal.**
- **Anchored, never travelling.** Travelling additionally destroys perceived intent. **Pause or jiggle keeps agency; wander loses it.**
- **Reactions fire 100–200 ms after the on-screen event, at fixed latency.** Jitter destroys perceived causality faster than lag. Make the reaction *stronger* than its cause — triggering rather than launching.
- **Colour features ≥ 1/8 of width (~17 px), contiguous, never on edges.** Silhouette outline must be luminance-carried and ≥ 2 px.

### Colour: the current encoding is on the wrong axis

**Valence as colour temperature at constant luminance is not supported, and the evidence against it is unusually clean.** Wilms & Oberfeld ran exactly this manipulation — three hues matched in L\* *and* luminance — and found hue explaining **ηp² = .588 of arousal** but **ηp² = .094 of valence, non-significantly (p = .051), with the sign reversing across saturation.**

The consequence is worse than "does not work": warm→cool will read as **intense→calm**, injecting an inverted duplicate of the arousal signal into a channel meant to be orthogonal — the exact failure ADR-0001 names as its own risk ("если каналы валентности и возбуждения визуально смешаются"). Options, by support:

1. **Reopen the constant-luminance constraint.** Lightness is the strongest valence carrier in every colour study (`Pleasure = .69·B`; ηp² = .491; LIGHT↔positive across 132 studies and 42,266 participants), and equiluminance separately degrades motion perception and spatial acuity. The reason for the constraint was sound (issue 04: dark blue vanished on a dark map) but *zero* luminance range overshot. A **bounded** range (e.g. sRGB luminance 110–190) keeps the buddy visible over dark forest and bright river alike while restoring a real valence channel.
2. **Demote colour temperature to a redundant arousal cue**, moving valence entirely into shape.
3. Keep it and accept that colour carries nothing reliable. Not recommended.

Independently: **do not use red for negative valence**, and **do not use a red↔green axis.**

### Can a faceless design compete? On arousal yes; on valence no.

**For faceless, and these are real.** Saerbeck & Bartneck found **no main effect of embodiment** — a Roomba disc and a robot cat head produced statistically identical affect attributions from identical motion parameters. Johnson et al. (1998): **contingency alone matched a face**; twelve-month-olds followed the "gaze" of an amorphous object that merely *reacted* within 1 s as reliably as a person's, while the object that moved but did not react **failed completely**. Heider & Simmel: 33 of 34 narrated three polygons as intentional beings.

**Against, and decisive for the second axis.** **Every faceless valence channel is weak, contested, or untested**: kinematics a flat null (p = .478); path curvature significant but non-monotonic with a failing regression; colour at constant luminance n.s. with a reversing sign; angularity real but modest (42% vs 28%) with a threat explanation that has failed several direct tests; relative phase best-supported but **never tested on an abstract shape**; non-rigid deformation **never parametrically studied at all**. **"A face is too small at 135 px" is empirically false** (Du & Martinez: ~20×30 px; Sinha: identity at 19×27 px). And **two dots are a measured, involuntary channel with no faceless equivalent** (Gao et al. 2010 Exp 3b, `p = .002`, unsuppressable on instruction) — no silhouette-only cue in this literature has been shown to do that.

**Verdict.** A faceless creature carries **arousal** at least as well as a faced one; the evidence is direct and the channel is robust to small size, eccentricity and compression. It cannot currently carry **valence** with anything better than a modest, contested angularity effect, because every other candidate is null, non-monotonic, or untested. The existing eye-based design (ADR-0001, issue 04) is on the right side of this: **lid geometry is a shape channel that survives at 135 px and gives valence a carrier faceless designs lack.**

If a faceless variant is built anyway, the two moves the literature supports are: **encode valence as angularity in the low-spatial-frequency silhouette** (a low-SF signal, so it survives, and decided in under 150 ms), and **encode valence as phase coherence between two or more deformation oscillators** — coherent for positive, counter-phase for negative — leaving their magnitude free for arousal. The second is the strongest theoretical bet available and also an unpublished experiment; **A/B it against the eye design with real viewers**, not on the strength of Pollick's phase-scrambling result alone.

### Cheapest high-value additions, independent of the face question

1. **Fixed-latency contingent reaction** (100–200 ms, jitter minimised). Best evidence-to-effort ratio in this document.
2. **Reaction magnitude exceeding its cause** — triggering, not launching.
3. **Arousal-scaled breathing, 0.17–0.50 Hz, 4–16 px, falling to zero at rest.**
4. **Pause and jiggle instead of drift** when idle. Free agency, zero irritation.
